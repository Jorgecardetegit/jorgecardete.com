Traditionally, **AI in healthcare** has been a [***‘black box’***](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_box), providing diagnosis or treatment recommendations without a clear explanation.

However, **Explainable AI (XAI)** is changing this narrative, especially in **cancer care** where understanding ***‘why’*** is as important as ***‘what’***.

**XAI** refers to artificial intelligence systems designed to provide **understandable explanations** of their **operations**, **decisions**, and **results**.

![Traditional vs Explainable AI](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:700/1*BsEo82Tl09JIuDgPh1_Mfg.png)
*Traditional vs Explainable AI | [Source](https://slideplayer.com/slide/11227478/)*

Its core objectives include:

- *Transparency*
- *Interpretability*
- *Trust and Confidence*

**Why do we need XAI?**
Consider the case of **lung cancer**, [the leading cause of cancer-related deaths globally](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/lung-cancer).

**AI systems** have been a game-changer in assisting and detecting this type of disease in [CT scans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CT_scan) with surprisingly **high accuracy**.

![Lung cancer detection with AI](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:627/0*U4KNYZvnHqmn6bih)
*Lung cancer detection with AI | [Source](https://www.itnonline.com/content/ai-analysis-can-improve-lung-cancer-detection-chest-radiographs)*

However, **XAI takes this one step further** by explaining what´s behind its diagnosis and highlighting specific patterns in the lung tissue**,** **providing oncologists with insights into the analytical process**.

### Okey, but if the algorithm is working who cares about how?

**Let´s see a practical example:**

Imagine a doctor using an AI system to **screen for lung cancer using patient CT scans**. The AI algorithm indicates that a patient is at high risk of lung cancer. However, **it doesn’t provide any explanation for its conclusion.**

The doctor finds challenging to understand this AI-generated decision.

He faces several issues:

- ***Uncertainty in decision-making:*** The doctor is hesitant to make a critical decision, based on an unexplained assessment.
- ***Difficulty in communicating with the patient:*** He struggles to explain to the patient why the AI has suggested a high risk of cancer.
- ***Lack of trust in the AI system:*** Without clear explanations, the doctor finds it hard to trust the AI’s assessment.
