When, [***I, Robot***](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I,_Robot_(film)) was released in **1950**, [Isaac Asimov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isaac_Asimov) had already published all **nine chapters** of the book as **separate short stories** for popular science fiction journals at that time.

Each story had its characters, some overlapping, but Asimov combined them into a coherent work, creating **one of the most famous science fiction novels of all time**.

- [*Robbie* ***(1940)***](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robbie_(short_story))
- [*Runaround* ***(1942)***](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Runaround_(story))
- [*Reason* ***(1941)***](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reason_(short_story))
- [*Catch That Rabbit* ***(1944)***](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catch_That_Rabbit)
- [*Liar!* ***(1941)***](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liar!_(short_story))
- [*Little Lost Robot* ***(1947)***](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Lost_Robot)
- [*Escape!* ***(1945)***](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Escape!)
- [*Evidence* ***(1946)***](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evidence_(short_story))
- [*The Evitable Conflict* ***(1950)***](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Evitable_Conflict)

Some of these stories would later be adapted for television and become the inspiration for the well-known [Will Smith movie](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0343818/).

### Runaround

But one of the narratives, ‘[***Runaround***](https://web.williams.edu/Mathematics/sjmiller/public_html/105Sp10/handouts/Runaround.html)***’***, could arguably be the most important science fiction short story of all time.

![Source: Runaround](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:540/0*4FMC5wuvDQ6ylKst)
*Source: Runaround*

It proposed the **Three Laws of Robots**:

- ***First Law.*** A robot may not injure a human being or, through inaction, allow a human being to come to harm.
- ***Second Law****.* A robot must obey the orders given to it by human beings except where such orders would conflict with the First Law.
- ***Third Law****.* A robot must protect its existence as long as such protection does not conflict with the First or Second Law.

**Nowadays**, the general consensus is that we are **still far away** from considering machines as **moral agents**.

> However, this reality is not so far away…

[In 2019, an algorithm affecting over 200 million people in US hospitals](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.aax2342) was found to favor **white patients over black patients** in predicting medical care needs.

It prioritized patients based on **healthcare spending**, a metric that didn’t accurately reflect the medical needs of all racial groups.

This led to certain racial groups receiving inappropriate healthcare services.

> This was a violation of **Asimov’s First Law of Robotics**.
